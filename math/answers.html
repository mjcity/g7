Do Now

Stephanie pays $12.95 for a 3 pound bag of almonds. What is the unit price of the almonds?

About $4.32 per pound

  1. A unit price is the cost of 1 pound.
  2. Divide the total cost by the number of pounds: 12.95 ÷ 3 = 4.31666…
  3. Round to the nearest cent. The third decimal digit is 6, so 4.31 rounds up to 4.32.
  4. The almonds cost about $4.32 per pound.

3 ÷ 12.95 gives pounds per dollar, rather than dollars per pound.

Worksheet page 2

Example: 3/5 = 12/20

Check the example proportion: 3/5 = 12/20.

True: the ratios are equivalent

  1. Find the cross products: 3 × 20 = 60 and 5 × 12 = 60.
  2. The cross products match, so the proportion is true.
  3. Another way: 3 × 4 = 12 and 5 × 4 = 20. Both parts use the same multiplier, 4.

Example: test a proportional relationship

Tell whether 5/6 and 12/15 demonstrate a proportional relationship. Step 1: Find the cross products.

No: 5/6 and 12/15 are not equivalent

  1. Multiply the numerator of the first ratio by the denominator of the second: 5 × 15 = 75.
  2. Multiply the other diagonal: 6 × 12 = 72.
  3. 75 ≠ 72, so the ratios do not form a true proportion.

Worksheet page 3

Example: 2/5 = 24/60

For 2/5 = 24/60: Step 1: 2 times what number gives me 24? Step 2: 5 times what number gives me 60? Is this value the same for both?

12 for both steps. Yes, the multiplier is the same, so the ratios are proportional.

  1. Find the first multiplier: 24 ÷ 2 = 12.
  2. Find the second multiplier: 60 ÷ 5 = 12.
  3. The two multipliers are equal.
  4. Therefore (2 × 12)/(5 × 12) = 24/60, so the ratios form a proportional relationship.

Proportional reasoning example, 1

Use proportional reasoning to see if the ratios form a proportional relationship: 3/4 = 12/16.

Yes: both parts are multiplied by 4

  1. To change 3 to 12, multiply by 4: 3 × 4 = 12.
  2. To change 4 to 16, also multiply by 4: 4 × 4 = 16.
  3. The multiplier is the same for both parts, so 3/4 and 12/16 are equivalent.
  4. Check: 3 × 16 = 48 and 4 × 12 = 48.

Proportional reasoning example, 2

Use proportional reasoning to see if the ratios form a proportional relationship: 10/20 = 30/70.

No: the two parts do not have the same multiplier

  1. To change 10 to 30, multiply by 3.
  2. If you multiply 20 by the same number, you get 20 × 3 = 60, not 70.
  3. The denominator would need to be 60 to match the multiplier 3, so the printed ratios are not equivalent.
  4. Check: 10 × 70 = 700, while 20 × 30 = 600. The cross products are different.

Example: solve for x

Solve for x: 8/12 = 6/x. The missing value is ____.

x = 9

  1. Cross multiply: 8 × x = 12 × 6.
  2. Calculate the known product: 8x = 72.
  3. Divide both sides by 8: x = 72 ÷ 8 = 9.
  4. Check: 8/12 simplifies to 2/3, and 6/9 also simplifies to 2/3.

The answer is 9. The value 72 is a cross product, not the missing denominator.

Missing-value example, 1

Use cross multiplication and/or proportional reasoning to find the missing value: x/10 = 10/20.

x = 5

  1. The denominator changes from 10 to 20 by multiplying by 2.
  2. The numerator must use the same multiplier: x × 2 = 10.
  3. Divide by 2: x = 5.
  4. Cross-product check: 5 × 20 = 100 and 10 × 10 = 100.

Missing-value example, 2

Find the missing value: 12/15 = 60/x.

x = 75

  1. To change 12 to 60, multiply by 5.
  2. Multiply the denominator by the same number: 15 × 5 = 75.
  3. So x = 75.
  4. Cross-multiplication check: 12 × 75 = 900 and 15 × 60 = 900.

Is 2/12 = 5/28 a true proportion?

No: the cross products are 56 and 60

  1. Find one cross product: 2 × 28 = 56.
  2. Find the other cross product: 12 × 5 = 60.
  3. 56 ≠ 60, so this is not a true proportion.

Practice, second question

Is 15/20 = 30/50 a true proportion?

No: the cross products are 750 and 600

  1. Find one cross product: 15 × 50 = 750.
  2. Find the other cross product: 20 × 30 = 600.
  3. 750 ≠ 600, so this is not a true proportion.
  4. You can also notice that 15 × 2 = 30, but 20 × 2 = 40, not 50.

Worksheet page 5

Solve for x: first proportion

Solve for x: x/11 = 3/33.

x = 1

  1. The right-hand denominator changes from 33 to 11 when you divide by 3.
  2. Do the same to the numerator: 3 ÷ 3 = 1, so 3/33 = 1/11.
  3. Now x/11 = 1/11, so x = 1.
  4. Check: 1 × 33 = 11 × 3 = 33. The cross-products are equal.

When simplifying, divide both numerator and denominator by the same number.

Solve for x: second proportion

Solve for x: 4/x = 8/12.

x = 6

  1. The numerator 8 becomes 4 when you divide by 2.
  2. Divide the denominator by 2 as well: 12 ÷ 2 = 6.
  3. So 8/12 = 4/6, which means x = 6.
  4. Check: 4 × 12 = 6 × 8 = 48.

Use the same scale factor for both parts of a ratio.

Proportional relationship: first pair

Use proportional reasoning to decide whether 25/30 and 100/110 form a proportional relationship.

No; the ratios are not equivalent.

  1. 25 × 4 = 100, so test a scale factor of 4.
  2. 30 × 4 = 120, not 110. The same scale factor does not work for both parts.
  3. Check the cross-products: 25 × 110 = 2,750 and 30 × 100 = 3,000.
  4. Since the cross-products differ, the ratios are not equivalent and the relationship is not proportional.

Both quantities getting larger is not enough. Their ratio must stay the same.

Proportional relationship: second pair

Use proportional reasoning to decide whether 9/12 and 36/48 form a proportional relationship.

Yes; the ratios are equivalent.

  1. 9 × 4 = 36 and 12 × 4 = 48.
  2. The same scale factor, 4, works for both parts, so the ratios are proportional.
  3. Check: 9 × 48 = 432 and 12 × 36 = 432. The cross-products are equal.
  4. Both ratios also simplify to 3/4.

Check both numerator and denominator before deciding.

Dog food: 3 cans

The unit rate is $2 per can of dog food. What would be the cost for 3 cans of dog food?

$6

  1. Each can costs $2.
  2. Cost = price per can × number of cans.
  3. $2 × 3 = $6, so 3 cans cost $6.

Dog food: 10 cans

At the same $2-per-can unit rate, what would be the cost for 10 cans of dog food?

$20

  1. The unit price stays $2 per can.
  2. Cost = $2 × 10 = $20.
  3. Check: $20 ÷ 10 cans = $2 per can.

Example 1: find the whole

60 is 75% of what number? Use a proportion to find your answer.

80

  1. Let n be the whole number. The part is 60 and the percent is 75%.
  2. Use part/whole = percent/100: 60/n = 75/100.
  3. Cross-multiply: 60 × 100 = 75n, so 6,000 = 75n.
  4. Divide both sides by 75: n = 80.
  5. Check: 0.75 × 80 = 60.

Do not find 75% of 60. The question already tells you that 60 is the 75% part.

Example 2: find the whole

20 is 25% of what number? Use a proportion to find your answer.

80

  1. Let n be the whole number.
  2. Write part/whole = percent/100: 20/n = 25/100.
  3. Cross-multiply: 20 × 100 = 25n, so 2,000 = 25n.
  4. Divide both sides by 25: n = 80.
  5. Check: 25% is one fourth, and one fourth of 80 is 20.

20 is already one fourth of the whole, so the whole must be larger than 20.

Example 3: lightning distance

If you hear thunder 15 seconds after you see lightning, you are about 3 miles from the lightning. What is your distance from the lightning if you hear thunder 10 seconds after you see lightning? Set up a proportion.

About 2 miles

  1. Let d be the distance in miles.
  2. Keep the units in the same order: 3 miles/15 seconds = d miles/10 seconds.
  3. Cross-multiply: 3 × 10 = 15d, so 30 = 15d.
  4. Divide by 15: d = 2. The distance is about 2 miles.
  5. Check: 15 ÷ 3 = 5 seconds per mile, and 10 ÷ 5 = 2 miles.

Do not reverse the unit order in only one fraction. The distance is approximate because the worksheet gives an approximate model.

Example 1: gasoline unit price

A customer bought 16 gallons of gasoline for $52. What is the price per gallon of the gasoline?

$3.25 per gallon

  1. Write dollars/gallons of gas = $52/16 gallons.
  2. Divide the total cost by the gallons: 52 ÷ 16 = 3.25.
  3. The unit-rate ratio is $3.25/1 gallon.
  4. Fill the blank with 3.25: The customer paid $3.25 per gallon.
  5. Check: 16 × $3.25 = $52.

16 ÷ 52 gives gallons per dollar, which is the reverse of what was asked.

Example 2: hourly pay

A grocery-store bagger worked for 20 hours last week and was paid $150. How much does the worker earn per hour?

$7.50 per hour

  1. Write dollars/hours = $150/20 hours.
  2. Divide: 150 ÷ 20 = 7.5.
  3. Write money with two decimal places: $7.50/1 hour.
  4. Fill the blank with 7.50: The worker earns $7.50 per hour.
  5. Check: 20 × $7.50 = $150.

$7.50 and $7.05 are different amounts. 7.5 dollars is 7 dollars and 50 cents.

Example 3: lever force

A person can lift a 10-pound weight by applying 30 pounds of force to a lever. If the required force is directly proportional to the weight lifted, how much force is needed to lift a 25-pound weight?

75 pounds of force

  1. Find the force per pound of lifted weight: 30 ÷ 10 = 3.
  2. The relationship requires 3 pounds of force for each pound of weight.
  3. For a 25-pound weight, multiply: 3 × 25 = 75.
  4. The person needs 75 pounds of force under the relationship given.

Keep force and lifted weight in matching positions in both ratios.

Worksheet page 8

Example 4: better plate deal

Sturdy’s paper plates cost $3.12 for 24 plates. Paperoni’s paper plates cost $4.32 for 36 paper plates. Which brand is the better deal? The better deal is the cheaper unit rate.

Paperoni’s is the better deal: $0.12 per plate, compared with Sturdy’s $0.13 per plate.

  1. Find Sturdy’s unit price: $3.12 ÷ 24 = $0.13 per plate.
  2. Find Paperoni’s unit price: $4.32 ÷ 36 = $0.12 per plate.
  3. Compare prices for the same unit: 12 cents per plate is less than 13 cents per plate.
  4. Choose Paperoni’s. It costs 1 cent less per plate.

The lower package price is not necessarily the better deal. Compare the unit prices.

State Test Question: discount and service fee

A concert ticket originally cost $75. A coupon gave a 20% discount, then the website applied a 10% service fee to the discounted price. The final ticket price was less than the original price by what percent?

C. 12%

  1. Find the discount: 20% of $75 = 0.20 × $75 = $15.
  2. Subtract it: $75 − $15 = $60.
  3. Find the service fee from the discounted price: 10% of $60 = 0.10 × $60 = $6.
  4. Add the fee: $60 + $6 = $66.
  5. Find the savings from the original price: $75 − $66 = $9.
  6. Divide savings by original price: $9 ÷ $75 = 0.12.
  7. Convert to a percent: 0.12 × 100% = 12%. Choose C.

Subtracting 10% from 20% does not work because the two percentages use different base prices.

What is an example of a proportional relationship?

Example: notebooks cost $3 each, so total cost C = 3n, where n is the number of notebooks.

  1. 1 notebook costs $3; 2 cost $6; 3 cost $9.
  2. For every nonzero number of notebooks, cost ÷ number = 3.
  3. The ratio is constant, so the relationship is proportional.

Other correct examples are acceptable.

2

What is the constant of proportionality?

The constant of proportionality is the fixed multiplier k in y = kx. It equals y ÷ x when x ≠ 0. In the notebook example, k = 3 dollars per notebook.

  1. Multiply each x-value by the same number to get y.
  2. That number is k, the constant of proportionality.
  3. For example, $6 ÷ 2 notebooks = $3 per notebook.

The question asks for a definition; the notebook example from question 1 illustrates it.

Ask Yourself

A percent is a ratio of a number to 100. How can that help you write one ratio in a proportion?

Write the percent as percent/100. Then set part/whole equal to percent/100.

  1. Percent means “per hundred,” so a percent p can be written as p/100.
  2. For example, 23% is 23/100.
  3. For 23% of 80, write x/80 = 23/100.

3

x/30 = 10/100; x = 3

  2. The whole is 30 and the percent is 10, so x/30 = 10/100.
  3. Cross-multiply: 100x = 30 × 10 = 300.
  4. Divide both sides by 100: x = 300 ÷ 100 = 3.

How could you set up a proportion to answer the question, “3 is 20% of what number”? Is the proportion different from the one you would use to find 20% of 3?

For “3 is 20% of what number?”, use 3/n = 20/100, so n = 15. Yes, it is different from “20% of 3”: that uses x/3 = 20/100, so x = 0.6.

  1. Use part/whole = percent/100 in both problems.
  2. In “3 is 20% of what number?”, 3 is the part and the whole n is unknown, so 3/n = 20/100.
  3. Cross-multiply: 3 × 100 = 20n, so 300 = 20n and n = 15.
  4. In “20% of 3”, 3 is the whole and the part x is unknown, so x/3 = 20/100.
  5. Cross-multiply: 100x = 3 × 20 = 60, so x = 0.6.
  6. The proportions are different because 3 is the part in the first question and the whole in the second.

A customer leaves a $3 tip at a restaurant. If that tip is equal to 15% of his total bill, what is his total bill?

$20

  2. Write part/whole = percent/100: 3/b = 15/100.
  3. Cross-multiply: 15b = 300. Divide by 15: b = 20.
